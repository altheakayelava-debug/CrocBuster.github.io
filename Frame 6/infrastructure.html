Infrastructure Projects

According to the University of the Philippines Center for Integrative and Development Studies (UP CIDS), corruption, mismanagement, and lack of proper planning are the main causes for how flood-control projects in the Philippines fail to protect communities. Despite the billions of pesos spent on dykes, riverbank protections, and drainage systems, investigations find that some projects were never completed, some were built with substandard materials, and others were overpriced or awarded to politically connected contractors. For example, certain riverbank embankments in Bulacan and Metro Manila were marked "finished" in official reports, while residents observed minimal to no actual construction at all. When storms and flooding become increasingly stronger due to climate change, these failed projects will leave communities vulnerable to damage in homes, livelihoods, and local infrastructure. The article states that without transparent planning, public participation, independent audits, and stricter oversight of procurement and contracting, flood-control measures might end up being another profit-oriented scheme instead of being truly protective of at-risk populations.

The article from the Thomson Reuters Foundation reported that despite the billions of pesos invested by the government in infrastructure projects such as dykes and seawalls to control seawater, many flood-control projects in the Philippines still failed to protect poor communities from the high tides. The main challenges were due to corruption, specifically overpriced contracts, ghost projects, and unfinished structures that were built with poor materials. Improper planning and research were not conducted. All these issues led to major flood-prone areas, especially in Bulacan, still highly vulnerable to flooding, hurting the livelihoods of Filipinos — especially those who live in poor communities. It also highlighted that advocacy groups such as Greenpeace Philippines call for transparency and more community involvement with nature-based solutions as well as a proper framework for flood-risk management to create effective long-term flood protection in the Philippines.
